Based on data collected through October 19, 2026, JobMarket Lens counts 144,868 postings published during its first two full weeks, October 5 to October 18, by 425 S&P 500 companies on their own career sites. Here is what stands out.

## Two weeks, 145,000 postings

The week of October 5 has 60,123 postings and the week of October 12 has 84,745. Part of that gap is how the data was gathered: the first full collection ran on October 16, so postings from the first week that had already closed by then were never seen. The mix of jobs was steady across both weeks, with sales roles at 19% and 21% and computer and mathematical roles at 9% and 10%.

## Sales and management lead the openings

One in five postings (20%) is for a sales or related role, many of them store and customer-facing jobs at large retailers. Management follows at 16%, then business and financial operations (11%), computer and mathematical occupations (9.3%) and architecture and engineering (8.3%).

The biggest posters are consumer-facing employers. Lowe's, Kroger, Starbucks, AutoZone, Ulta Beauty and CVS Health each list more than 3,000 postings, followed by Amazon and JPMorgan Chase. Each collection reads at most the newest 3,000 postings per company, so the largest counts may be understated.

## A third of postings disclose pay

33% of all postings, and 44% of U.S. postings, state a pay range in U.S. dollars. The median midpoint of those ranges, annualized, is $90,152. It varies widely by occupation:

| Occupation group | Postings with pay | Median midpoint |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Management | 8,493 | $156,000 |
| Computer and mathematical | 5,021 | $152,955 |
| Architecture and engineering | 4,183 | $125,427 |
| Business and financial operations | 5,085 | $95,730 |
| Healthcare practitioners and technical | 2,820 | $84,881 |
| Sales and related | 9,139 | $50,107 |
| Transportation and material moving | 1,644 | $47,279 |

> Posted ranges are what employers advertise, annualized for comparison (hourly × 2,080). They are not actual earnings.

## Degrees: a split between bachelor's and high school

Just over half of postings (74,230) state a minimum degree. Of those, 49% ask for a bachelor's degree and 43% for a high school diploma; master's degrees (2.6%) and doctorates (2.0%) are rare as minimums. Only 16% of postings that state a degree say equivalent experience is accepted instead.

## Majors: engineering, computer science and business

30,831 postings name a field of study, about four in ten of those that state a degree. Engineering of any kind is named most often (10,265 postings), followed by computer science (8,498), business and management (8,134) and electrical and computer engineering (5,845). Many postings list several acceptable majors, and many more ask only for "a related field".

## Skills: communication first, data close behind

Communication is mentioned in 70% of postings and collaboration in 59%. Among specific skills, data analysis appears in 23% of postings, well beyond technology roles.

## Credentials that open doors

A security clearance is the most frequently named credential (6,222 postings), reflecting defense and aerospace employers. It is followed by a driver's license (3,651), a commercial driver's license (3,420), a registered nurse license (1,859), the PMP project management certification (1,597) and the CPA (1,185).

## Remote work is the exception

Of the 63,419 postings that state a work arrangement, 37% are remote or hybrid and 63% are on-site. Fully remote postings are just 9%.

## Where the jobs are

67% of postings are in the United States; most of the rest reflect these companies' global workforces. Within the U.S., California (9,473), Texas (9,267), Florida (4,930) and New York (4,542) have the most postings. Among postings that state experience, the median requirement is 4 years.

## About these numbers

Postings measure hiring intent, not hires, and coverage is not complete: some career sites block automated access and are shown as blocked rather than estimated. See the [Methodology](/about/methodology) for how each field is derived, or explore the numbers yourself on the [dashboard](/).
